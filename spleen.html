Spleen Health

1. Splenomegaly (Enlarged Spleen) (CKD)

Definition: A condition where the spleen becomes larger than normal due to infections, liver disease, or blood disorders.

Symptoms:

  • Pain or fullness in the upper left abdomen.
  • Feeling full quickly after eating.
  • Fatigue and anemia.

Precautions:

  • Treat underlying infections and diseases.
  • Avoid contact sports to prevent spleen rupture.
  • Maintain a healthy diet and lifestyle.

2. Hypersplenism

Symptoms:

  • Low red blood cells (anemia), leading to fatigue.
  • Low white blood cells, increasing infections.
  • Low platelets, causing easy bruising or bleeding.

Precautions:

  • Regular blood tests to monitor blood cell levels.
  • Treat the underlying cause (such as infections or liver disease).
  • Avoid infections and get vaccinations.

3. Spleen Rupture

Definition: A medical emergency where the spleen tears, usually due to trauma or an enlarged spleen.

Symptoms:

  • sudden, sharp pain in the left upper abdomen.
  • Dizziness and fainting due to blood loss.
  • Low blood pressure and rapid heartbeat.

Precautions:

  • Avoid high-contact sports if you have an enlarged spleen.
  • Seek immediate medical attention after an injury to the left abdomen.
  • Wear seat belts properly to reduce injury risk.

4. Spleen Infarction

Definition: A condition where blood flow to the spleen is blocked, causing part of the spleen tissue to die

Symptoms:

  • Sudden left upper abdominal pain.
  • Fever and nausea.
  • Enlarged spleen and tenderness.

Precautions

  • Manage conditions like sickle cell disease or blood clots.
  • Stay hydrated and maintain a healthy circulatory system.
  • Regular medical checkups for early detection.

5. Autoimmune Hemolytic Anemia (AIHA)

Definition: A condition where the immune system attacks red blood cells, causing the spleen to remove them too quickly

Symptoms:

  • Fatigue and weakness.
  • Jaundice (yellowing of the skin).
  • Dark urine and enlarged spleen.

Precautions:

  • Treat autoimmune conditions early.
  • Regular blood tests and checkups.
  • Avoid cold exposure (in some forms of AIHA).

6. Sickle Cell Disease (Splenic Sequestration Crisis)

Definition: A condition where sickle-shaped red blood cells block blood flow in the spleen, leading to a sudden enlargement.

Symptoms:

  • Sudden spleen enlargement.
  • Severe anemia, causing weakness and dizziness.
  • Pain in the left abdomen.

Precautions:

  • Stay hydrated to prevent blood thickening.
  • Take prescribed medications like hydroxyurea.
  • Seek immediate medical attention for severe symptoms.

7. Gaucher's Disease

Definition: A genetic disorder where fatty substances build up in the spleen and liver, causing organ enlargement

Symptoms:

  • Enlarged spleen and liver.
  • Easy bruising and anemia.
  • Bone pain and fractures.

Precautions:

  • Early diagnosis and enzyme replacement therapy.
  • Maintain a healthy diet and lifestyle.
  • Regular monitoring of blood and bone health.

8. Felty's Syndrome

Definition: A rare complication of rheumatoid arthritis, where the spleen enlarges and white blood cell count drops

Symptoms:

  • Swollen spleen.
  • Frequent infections due to low white blood cells.
  • Joint pain and swelling.

Precautions:

  • Manage rheumatoid arthritis with proper treatment.
  • Strengthen immunity with a healthy lifestyle.
  • Regular blood tests to monitor white blood cells.

9. Lymphoma (Cancer of the Spleen)

Definition: A type of cancer that affects the lymphatic system, including the spleen.

Symptoms:

  • swollen spleen causing left upper abdominal pain.
  • Unexplained weight loss.
  • Night sweats and fever.

Precautions:

  • o Regular cancer screenings if at risk.
  • o Avoid exposure to harmful chemicals and radiation.
  • o Maintain a strong immune system.

10. Infectious Mononucleosis ("Mono" or Epstein-Barr Virus Infection)

Definition: A viral infection that can cause spleen enlargement.

Symptoms:

  • Fatigue and sore throat.
  • Swollen lymph nodes and spleen.
  • Swollen lymph nodes and spleen.

Precautions:

  • Avoid sharing drinks and utensils to prevent infection.
  • Get plenty of rest and stay hydrated.
  • Avoid physical activities to prevent spleen rupture.
